PMFBY & WBCIS Underwriting Architecture

Domain knowledge, operational framework, actuarial metrics, and underwriting strategies in Indian agricultural insurance.

1. Scheme Overview & Mandate

The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) and Weather Based Crop Insurance Scheme (WBCIS) provide comprehensive risk coverage for crops against non-preventable natural risks (drought, flood, unseasonal rainfall, pests, diseases). Farmers pay a nominal uniform premium (2% for Kharif foodgrains/oilseeds, 1.5% for Rabi foodgrains/oilseeds, and 5% for Annual Commercial/Horticultural crops). The remainder is shared equally (50:50) as premium subsidy between the State and Central Governments.

2. Actuarial Metrics Derivations

  • Total Premium ($P$): Sum of Farmer Share + State Subsidy + Central Government Subsidy.
  • Burn Rate / Loss Ratio ($BR$): Ratio of total claims paid to total premium collected:
    $$BR = rac{ ext{Claims Paid}}{ ext{Total Premium}}$$ A Burn Rate < 100% indicates underwriting profitability; > 100% represents underwriting deficit.
  • Premium Rate ($Rate$): Ratio of gross premium to Sum Insured:
    $$Rate = rac{ ext{Total Premium}}{ ext{Sum Insured}}$$
  • Cup & Cap Model (80:110): In recent tender rounds, states utilize risk-sharing corridors:
    • Claims < 80% of Premium: Insurer refunds surplus back to Government.
    • Claims 80% to 110%: Insurer absorbs full risk.
    • Claims > 110%: Government provides extra indemnity subsidy or reinsurance stop-loss.

3. Portfolio Cluster Optimization

State governments package districts into multi-district clusters for competitive insurance bidding. A well-designed cluster combines historically low-volatility (irrigated) districts with high-risk (rainfed) districts to achieve:

  • Geographical risk pooling and non-correlation of climatic shocks.
  • Lower aggregate coefficient of variation (CV) for the underwriting portfolio.
  • Balanced premium volumes that meet capital solvency requirements.
